01

National data

National Hospitalisation Trends (2011–2021)

Research question

How did the annual number of hospitalised injuries from road crashes in Australia change between 2011 and 2021, and what notable patterns emerged during this period?

Key findings

Annual hospitalisations rose from 34,033 in 2011 to a peak of 39,866 in 2019 (+17.1%). They fell to 37,966 in 2020 (-4.8%) before recovering to 39,505 in 2021 (+4.1% year on year; 16.1% above 2011).

02

Road user groups

Road User Categories and Contributions to Change

Research question

How did hospitalised injury counts and their proportions differ across road user types, and which groups contributed most to the changes between selected years?

Key findings

In 2021, car drivers accounted for the largest share (13,327 hospitalisations; 33.7%), followed by motorcyclists (8,883; 22.5%). From 2011 to 2021, pedal cyclists had the largest count increase (+2,772; +51.4%; share 15.8% to 20.7%), while pedestrian hospitalisations fell by 420 (-15.3%; share 8.1% to 5.9%).

03

Age groups

Age-Group Distributions Over Time

Research question

How did the distribution of hospitalised road crash injuries vary across age groups in Australia, and how did these distributions change between 2011 and 2021?

Key findings

People aged 40–64 were the largest group in both 2011 (10,660 hospitalisations; 31.3%) and 2021 (13,026; 33.0%). For ages 17–25, counts edged down from 8,094 to 8,010, and their share fell from 23.8% to 20.3% (-3.5 percentage points).

04

States and territories

State and Territory Comparisons

Research question

How did hospitalised road crash injury counts change across Australian states and territories from 2011 to 2021, and which states showed the largest changes between 2018 and 2021?

Key findings

From 2018 to 2021, Queensland had the largest increase: 9,381 to 10,447 hospitalisations (+1,066; +11.4%). New South Wales had the largest count decrease, from 9,794 to 9,003 (-791; -8.1%); the Northern Territory had the steepest percentage decline (-14.7%; 832 to 710).

05

First Nations data

First Nations Peoples Road Crash Injuries

Research question

How did recorded hospitalised road crash injuries among First Nations Peoples change between 2011 and 2021, and how did the patterns differ among car occupants, motorcyclists, pedal cyclists and pedestrians?

Key findings

Counts increased in all four groups from 2011 to 2021: the combined car driver/passenger/unknown-position category rose from 617 to 1,158 (+87.7%), motorcyclists from 187 to 520 (+178.1%), pedal cyclists from 121 to 207 (+71.1%), and pedestrians from 180 to 235 (+30.6%). Motorcyclists had the largest percentage rise, while the combined car category had the largest increase in count (+541); these are counts, not population-adjusted rates.

About the data

This story uses prepared extracts from BITRE’s Australian road-crash hospitalisation data, covering 2011–2021. Hospitalisations are not equivalent to crashes or people injured, and counts alone do not account for population size, exposure, or changes in reporting.
