THE OVERALL PICTURE

National Trends

Research question: How did the annual number of hospitalised injuries from road crashes in Australia change between 2011 and 2021, and what notable patterns emerged during this period?

Recorded hospitalisations increased by 16.1% across the period. The highest annual total was 39,866 in 2019, followed by a drop in 2020.

AGE GROUPS

Age-Group Distributions Over Time

Research question: How did the distribution of hospitalised road crash injuries vary across age groups in Australia, and how did these distributions change between 2011 and 2021?

The 40-64 group made up 31.32% of recorded hospitalisations in 2011 and 32.97% in 2021. The 17-25 group's share declined from 23.78% to 20.28%.

WHERE DID PATTERNS DIFFER?

States and Territories

Research question: How did hospitalised road crash injury counts change across Australian states and territories from 2011 to 2021, and which states showed the largest changes between 2018 and 2021?

Queensland increased by 1,066 hospitalisations (11.36%) from 2018 to 2021. The Northern Territory had the largest percentage decline (14.66%).

05

FOUR SELECTED ROAD USER GROUPS

First Nations Peoples

Research question: How did recorded hospitalised road crash injuries among First Nations Peoples change between 2011 and 2021, and how did the patterns differ among car occupants, motorcyclists, pedal cyclists and pedestrians?

All four selected groups recorded higher counts in 2021 than in 2011. Motorcyclists had the largest relative increase, while car occupants had the largest count in both years.

Hospitalisations by road user, 2011-2021

Number of hospitalisations

This analysis covers four selected road-user categories with published values. It does not represent all First Nations hospitalisations. Unpublished values elsewhere in the original workbook were not estimated.

About the data and its limits

The figures are recorded hospitalisations from road traffic crashes, not the number of crashes and not population-adjusted injury rates. A higher count may reflect population size, exposure, or reporting practices rather than a higher individual risk.

BITRE reports changes to hospital admission criteria in Victoria in 2012 and New South Wales in 2017. Some First Nations cells are not published, and age records contain one “Missing” category in 2014. We have not substituted zero for unpublished values.

Sources: BITRE Hospitalised Injury and Hospitalised Injury Data Dictionary (September 2023).
